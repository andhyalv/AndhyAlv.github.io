Using a dataset from the Massachusetts Education Department, I created KPI visualizations in Tableau to get a clear picture of how their education system is performing.


### Introduction
A strong education is an important foundation for kids as they grow up. I believe it is important to understand why schools perform the way they do, and to take the lessons from that to improve overall performance. Through analyzing the Massachusetts DOE dataset, I believe that we will we learn insights about the low and high performing schools. These insights will allow key stakeholders to make informed decisions on how to improve performance in the education system. 

### What will you learn
a) The graduation rate among schools is split by many high performing schools, with several other low performing schools.

b) Schools with high college attendance rate have a class size around 10-20 students, and economically disadvantaged schools tend to have much lower college attendance rates.

c) Many schools are behind the passing threshold for the 4th grade standardized math test.

### Dataset and tools
The dataset is provided by the Massachusetts Department of Education. It contains performance metrics from 1,861 schools and 953,748 students. I input this data into Tableau to create visualizations for the relationships that we are investigating. ### Data Analysis
The sample size of the dataset includes 1,861 schools and 953,748 students. We will investigate a few KPI's to get a clear picture on the performance of the education system.

#### a) Graduation Rate
Taking a look at the graduation rate among schools, there is a noticeable difference between the lowest performing schools and the highest performing schools with a 100% graduation rate. The trend begs the question of why are these differences taking place?

The next steps are to investigate the characteristics of schools with low graduation rates and those with higher graduation rates. Through the [dashboard](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/andhy.alvarez/viz/Book2_17530197870140/Dashboard1?publish=yes) you can hover over the bar chart to see the name of the school. From there you can dig deeper and learn more about each individual school to answer our research question.

#### b) The Relationship between College Attendance and Class Size
In this next relationship we see that there is a sweet spot for average class size and high attendance rates of colleges. The best performing average class size seems to be between 15-25 students. If we look further, there are a lot of low college attendance rates in schools that also have this average class size. So this makes me wonder why is that the case? 

Our question leads us to look into what other variables affect college attendance rate. I decided to look at economic status among schools. In the graph, darker red bubbles represent schools with a low % of economic disadvantage among the students, and the dark blue bubbles show a high % of economic disadvantage. A trend emerges of schools with higher economic disadvantage also having lower college attendance rates. Hovering over each school in this area will give us the opportunity to research deeper and see what other factors are involved in the low college attendance rate. 

#### c) 4th Grade Math Levels
The line chart shows that there are many schools who do not meet the standards for the 4th Grade Math Levels. Any of the points in red represent schools that do not meet the passing threshold, while the blue points are schools that meet the passing threshold. We can single out schools in the red line for focused research and allocate more resources to improve their performance.  

### Conclusion
From the dataset, we get a clear picture of performance in the Massachusetts Education system. Here are some takeaways from the data.

1) We learn that graduation rates show a trend of many high performing schools compared to low performing ones. We can dig deeper into specific low performing schools to understand why that is the case.

2) We also learned that economic status plays a large role in college attendance rates, with schools at an economic disadvantage being less likely to have college attendees. 

3) Lastly we learned that many schools are not meeting the passing threshold for 4th Grade Math levels. Digging deeper into why this is the case is important because it can provide us with solutions that can improve the educational foundation of students.
